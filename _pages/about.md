I’m a computational biologist studying how the human microbiome shapes infectious disease. My research focuses on HIV and SARS-CoV-2, where I integrate multi-omic and spatial data to understand how the microbes we carry interact with the immune system to influence disease outcomes. I then test these computational findings in the “wet” lab, using bacterial and mammalian cell culture to uncover the mechanisms behind them. I recently completed my PhD in Bioinformatics and Computational Biology at the University of Minnesota, where my dissertation examined the drivers of chronic inflammation in people with HIV on antiretroviral therapy. I am currently interested in how gut microbes influence the HIV reservoir and latency reversal, understanding why people with HIV have higher risk of cardiovascular disease, and how AI can accelerate discovery in microbiome research.
