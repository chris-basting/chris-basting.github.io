I'm a computational biologist who studies how the microbiome, metabolism, and the immune system interact in human disease. I recently completed my PhD in Bioinformatics and Computational Biology at the University of Minnesota, where my dissertation investigated the drivers of chronic inflammation in people with HIV on antiretroviral therapy.

My work combines wet-lab and computational approaches. I build bioinformatics pipelines that integrate multi-omic data (microbiome sequencing, targeted and untargeted metabolomics, cytokine profiling, and spatial transcriptomics) and apply them to clinical cohorts to find biomarkers and mechanisms of disease. Alongside HIV, I've contributed to studies of COVID-19, Zika virus, and recurrent bacterial vaginosis, and have published in journals including *JCI Insight*, *Scientific Reports*, *Microbiology Spectrum*, and *The Journal of Infectious Diseases*.

Since 2020, I've managed the Klatt Lab at the University of Minnesota, overseeing daily operations, budgets, and regulatory compliance while mentoring graduate and undergraduate researchers. Before that, I worked in industry at Diversigen, running next-generation sequencing microbiome projects from sample intake to client delivery, and at NatureWorks, developing and optimizing fermentation processes.

I'm currently looking for roles in computational biology, bioinformatics, and translational research. Feel free to [reach out](mailto:bast0159@umn.edu) or [download my CV](/files/Basting_CV.pdf).
